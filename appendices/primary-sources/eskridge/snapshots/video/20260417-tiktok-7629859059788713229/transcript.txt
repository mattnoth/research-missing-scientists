Just before her death in 2022, Amy Escridge had announced plans to present her groundbreaking work in anti-gravity technology. The 34-year-old had been openly experimenting in the field and founded a research company called the Institute for Exotic Science to establish a more public space for developments in anti-gravity work. Her death from a supposed self-inflicted gunshot wound has long been questioned since she had expressed fearing for her life at the time. She worked closely with her father. According to NASA, Richard Escridge is a retired engineer whose many projects included novel work on a system to propel spacecraft with high-speed plasmoids. In speaking with him, Escridge dismissed any link between the death of his daughter to the 10 other scientists who mysteriously died or gone missing over the past few years. All had links to government secrets and programs tied to military defense and UFOs. Richard Escridge did not want to be interviewed, but he told me on the phone, scientists also die just like other people. Nonetheless, suspicion abounds.
