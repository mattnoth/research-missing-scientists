# Robert Bartholomew, Ph.D.

## Credentials
Medical sociologist, author, and journalist; longtime researcher of mass psychogenic illness, moral panics, and collective delusion. Honorary senior lecturer in the Department of Psychological Medicine, University of Auckland. Has written extensively on the sociology of extraordinary beliefs and pattern-perception errors. Writes the "It's Catching" blog at *Psychology Today*.

## Relevance Assessment
Direct methodological expertise on the *interpretive* question at the center of this dossier — namely, whether an apparent cluster reflects a real causal pattern or is an artifact of human pattern-seeking. Bartholomew does not claim inside knowledge of any individual case; his contribution is a base-rate / cognitive-bias reframing of the whole phenomenon. This is the most credentialed on-the-record articulation of the skeptic ("Scully-side") position located to date, and it speaks to the dossier's single largest open analytical gap: the never-computed base rate.

## On-the-Record Statements

### April 22, 2026 — *Psychology Today* ("The Mystery of the Dead and Missing Research Scientists")
Bartholomew argues the apparent pattern is an example of **apophenia** — the perception of meaningful connections among unrelated events. He frames the "wave of dead scientists" narrative as driven by pattern-seeking and confirmation bias rather than evidence of coordinated targeting.

- On pattern-perception: "The human brain is wired to seek out patterns — an adaptation that helped our ancestors to detect predators stalking in tall grass."
- On confirmation bias: people notice "evidence that supports our beliefs, while ignoring or downplaying evidence that does not."
- On base rates: emphasizes that the U.S. has thousands of nuclear and aerospace scientists, and that deaths in large populations are statistically inevitable; individually unremarkable deaths acquire a "sinister" quality only when retroactively linked by narrative.
- Cites specific cases as illustrations of mundane explanations being available: Michael Hicks (JPL; died at 59 with pre-existing medical issues), Nuno Loureiro (MIT; killed in a December 2025 mass shooting by a gunman with a personal grudge), and Carl Grillmair (Caltech/NASA; killed outside his LA home in what investigators treated as a crime of opportunity).

## Documented Conflicts of Interest or Professional Positioning
No financial or institutional stake in the outcome. Professional positioning strongly favors the null hypothesis: Bartholomew's body of work is built on demonstrating that perceived clusters and collective fears are usually psychological/sociological artifacts, which predisposes him toward a debunking frame. That prior is a source of interpretive bias in the skeptical direction, and his article offers cognitive-bias reasoning rather than case-specific investigation — but the base-rate logic it raises is a legitimate and, in this dossier, still-unanswered challenge.
